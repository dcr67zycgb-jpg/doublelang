How to Prepare for the TORFL Exam in 2026

We break down the structure of the Test of Russian as a Foreign Language, what each section checks, which level to choose, and a step-by-step plan for preparing from scratch.

📅 2026⏱ 8 min read✍️ DoubleLang

TORFL (Test of Russian as a Foreign Language) is a state certification exam that confirms a foreign citizen's level of Russian. The certificate is recognized by Russian universities and is required for admission, citizenship, and employment in certain fields.

Let's look at how the exam is structured in 2026, which level to choose, and how to build a preparation plan — even if you're starting from zero.

What Is TORFL and Why You Need It

The TORFL system includes six levels — from elementary to the highest (TORFL-IV) — corresponding to the CEFR scale from A1 to C2. The certificate has no expiry date and is officially recognized.

  • Study: admission to a Russian university program taught in Russian usually requires TORFL-I (B1).
  • Russian citizenship: you must confirm knowledge of Russian — typically a basic level.
  • Work: work patents and permits for migrants require passing a combined exam on Russian language, Russian history, and the basics of legislation.

💡 Important: labor migrants and university applicants sit different exams — check exactly which certificate you need for your goal.

Exam Structure: 4 Sections

At every TORFL level, the exam consists of four subtests. To get the certificate you must pass the minimum score in each section — you can't compensate for a weak section with a strong one.

1. Vocabulary and Grammar

Multiple-choice tasks on choosing the correct word form, case, or verb aspect. Tests knowledge of the Russian grammatical system.

2. Reading

Texts of varying difficulty — from announcements to journalistic pieces. You need to grasp the main idea and find details.

3. Listening

Dialogues, announcements, and broadcast excerpts. Tests understanding of speech at natural speed, including colloquial expressions.

4. Writing and Speaking

Writing a letter or an essay on a given topic; an oral interview with the examiner — monologue and dialogue.

⚠️ Passing score: you must meet the minimum in every subtest. Failing one section means retaking only that section within a set period.

Which Level to Choose

  • Elementary / Basic (A1–A2) — for everyday communication.
  • TORFL-I (B1) — the "gold standard": required for university admission and a work patent.
  • TORFL-II (B2) — fluent communication, working with texts in your field.
  • TORFL-III–IV (C1–C2) — professional and philological level, needed for teaching and academic work in Russian.

How Long Preparation Takes From Scratch

Russian is considered a challenging language because of its case system and verb aspects, but with structured lessons progress is fast. Benchmarks with 2–3 lessons a week with a teacher:

  • From zero to A2: 4–6 months.
  • From zero to B1 (TORFL-I): 9–12 months.
  • From zero to B2 (TORFL-II): about 1.5–2 years.
  • From A2 to B1: 4–5 months of focused preparation.

Step-by-Step Preparation Plan

Step 1. Learn the Cyrillic Alphabet and Basic Phonetics

Reading Cyrillic takes 1–2 weeks to master. After that, it's important to get pronunciation right early — especially stress, which is not fixed in Russian.

Step 2. Build the System of Cases and Verb Aspects

This is the core of Russian grammar and the main source of mistakes for foreigners. Without a systematic breakdown of the six cases and perfective/imperfective aspect, a high score on grammar and writing is out of reach.

Step 3. Practice Under Time Pressure

Work through official TORFL sample tests for your level with a timer — pacing across every section matters on exam day.

Step 4. Work with a Teacher on Writing and Speaking

These sections are the hardest to prepare for alone. A teacher familiar with TORFL criteria will help you structure your writing, expand vocabulary, and feel more confident in the oral interview.
